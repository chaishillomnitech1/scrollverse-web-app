# Phase 444 — Knowledge Commons Initial Source Index

**Record date:** 2026-08-18 (EDT)  
**Purpose:** Preserve the first verified public-source pathways for the ScrollVerse Knowledge Commons. Each pathway is for reader-directed inspection; it does not settle religious, historical, cultural, or financial questions on behalf of visitors.

## Directly verified institutional pathways

| Library lane | Source | Access and scope | Limitation and context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dead Sea Scrolls and Second Temple research | [Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library][1] — Israel Antiquities Authority | The public library offers featured scrolls, archive exploration, educational material, and high-resolution spectral images of manuscript fragments. | The visible archive is an institutional digital library and learning surface; visitors should treat fragment interpretation and translation as source- and edition-specific. |
| Ancient Near Eastern and cuneiform research | [Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative][2] | The public collection provides browse and search paths for publications, collections, periods, transliterations, translations, artifact metadata, documentation, and highlighted objects. | Artifact records, transliterations, translations, and editorial reconstructions require source-aware reading and should not be reduced to one universal historical conclusion. |
| Financial history and economic institutions | [FRASER — Federal Reserve Archival System for Economic Research][3] — Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis | The public archive supports browsing by title, author, subject, and date, and exposes historical materials such as central-bank history, Treasury publications, and economic records. | The archive’s holdings and institutional context are primarily U.S.-focused; a historical record is an educational source rather than investment, trading, payment, token, or credit advice. |
| Jewish texts and translation study | [Sefaria][4] | The nonprofit public library presents Jewish texts, translations, and commentaries, with browseable lanes including Tanakh, Mishnah, Talmud, Midrash, Halakhah, Kabbalah, liturgy, Jewish thought, and Second Temple materials. | Individual texts, translations, and annotations have their own editorial and interpretive context; the card introduces a library pathway rather than representing a single translation or interpretation as definitive. |
| Qurʾān manuscript and text-history research | [Corpus Coranicum][5] | The academy project provides public research pathways for printed editions, manuscript witnesses, variant readings, related texts, commentary, and concordance. | Its historical-critical project context is one research approach; the source card must make that approach visible and should not substitute it for religious teaching, faith, or the full range of Muslim scholarly traditions. |

## Source-card operating note

Both sources are available as external, reader-directed research pathways. ScrollVerse can identify and link them with their source organization, access purpose, and limitation. It should not reproduce protected images or translations in full without a specific access-rights review.
