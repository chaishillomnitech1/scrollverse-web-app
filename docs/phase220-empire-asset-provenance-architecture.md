# Empire-Wide Asset and Digital Twin Architecture

**Status:** Source-labeled design and browser-local planning architecture. It creates no rights registration, encryption, signature, asset registry, token, product, company record, manufacturer arrangement, sale, payment, or custody operation.

| Asset family | Required evidence family | Digital-twin representation | Tokenization readiness boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Company / brand | Exact legal/public record where available, mark/goods context, and source/date. | A public-safe operating and release-context map, not a legal entity or authorized representative. | Not evaluated until a specific original asset and reviewed buyer promise are identified. |
| Clothing line | Original-design source, mark review, garment/sample specification, production and fulfillment requirements. | A design-to-product lineage record linking collection, design version, sample, SKU, and release status. | Optional authenticity/access record only after product and rights evidence are complete. |
| Merchandise / phygital | Artwork, specification, quality/fulfillment, SKU, shipping/return terms, and consumer support evidence. | A physical/digital package record that keeps fulfillment separate from a collectible concept. | Token does not replace delivery, warranty, return, or consumer obligations. |
| Music / media | Source/master/contributor context, metadata, cover/media rights, accessible format, and release package. | A versioned work/release lineage with source and distribution states. | Edition eligibility is separate from music delivery and rights. |
| Books / comics / stories | Originality/contributor record, manuscript/script version, audience/accessibility plan, license, and release package. | A story-world or edition map that does not claim character or title rights without evidence. | Requires exact artifact, buyer promise, metadata, and review. |
| Code / digital products | Repository/version/license metadata, dependency/security review, public source, and operating status. | A documented technical artifact map, not a claim of deployment or security. | A repository, hash, or URL alone is not a commercial or token entitlement. |
| Collectible / token concept | Cleared release package, defined buyer promise, metadata policy, contract/security path, and consumer terms. | A concept-to-evidence matrix that distinguishes review eligibility from a live chain record. | Fail-closed until every launch gate has an actual reviewed record. |

## Rose-Gold Signature Policy

The rose-gold signature is an intentionally visible **design marker** for a ScrollVerse source/evidence route. It is not encryption or a cryptographic signature. A real integrity or signature record must separately name the exact artifact version, method/algorithm, digest/signature value, verification source, and result. Under NIST’s Digital Signature Standard, a digital signature is used to detect unauthorized modification and authenticate a claimed signatory; it is not, by itself, a legal ownership or title determination. [1]

## Public Evidence Labels

Use `publicly observable record`, `owner-supplied context`, `official external record`, and `missing or needs review`. A route should identify the date/context and the limitation of every label. The current application never treats a self-published route, a visual seal, a repository, a file name, a hash, or a token concept as sufficient proof of every other property.

## Clothing and Company Operating Rule

A clothing/company project should be decomposed into separate records: original design/artwork, name/mark, garment or product specification, sample/quality state, manufacturing/fulfillment relationship, SKU/inventory decision, consumer terms, and optional collector record. The USPTO explains that federal trademark searching is an important clearance step but cannot itself provide a clear-cut answer about registrability. [2]
