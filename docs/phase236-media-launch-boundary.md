## Campaign Principle

> **Reach is earned by relevance, clarity, and consent—not by volume alone.**

The campaign package will focus on specific story angles, genuine editorial fit, and a trackable review path. Each record must be source-labeled and have a practical status: research candidate, qualified public contact path, owner-review required, outreach approved, contacted, response received, or do-not-contact. The package does not create or imply affiliation, endorsement, coverage, paid placement, guaranteed virality, or a business outcome.

## Operational Boundary

No external media contact, email address, personal data, Discord member, CRM record, list subscription, social post, direct message, email, outreach batch, analytics event, or community action has been collected, imported, created, contacted, posted, or sent in this phase. Linked Gmail URLs contained in the supplied material are not opened or used.

The next work block will deliver empty templates and review-ready copy only. If the owner later chooses to connect a CRM or inbox, that integration must be scoped separately with its purpose, data fields, retention, sender identity, approval owner, unsubscribe/withdrawal route, and audit process made visible before any contact data is handled.

## Research Findings Applied

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission explains that commercial email rules apply beyond bulk sending and do not contain a business-to-business exception. Its guidance calls for accurate sender/routing information, truthful subject lines, a valid postal address, a clear opt-out mechanism, and prompt honoring of opt-outs.[1] The launch kit therefore includes sender identity, campaign classification, physical-address placeholder, opt-out channel, do-not-contact state, and a response/stop date rather than relying on a generic “sent” field.

For contacts in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner’s Office states that marketing email or texts to individuals generally require specific consent unless a limited existing-customer exception applies, and it recommends retaining a do-not-contact list. It further notes that direct messages via social media fall within its broad electronic-mail framing.[2] The database will not assume that a public-looking personal corporate address or social account is a blanket marketing permission; it will record contact type, source, consent basis, and exclusion status for owner review.

Discord’s Community Guidelines prohibit unsolicited bulk messages and artificially inflated engagement, while its community guidance recommends rules, transparent enforcement, moderator alignment, and channel-specific expectations.[3] The community brief therefore excludes raids, unsolicited DMs, automated posting, or engagement manipulation. It adopts a request-first approach: contribute only where a server’s rules allow it, ask moderators for the appropriate channel, and leave when promotion is unwelcome.
