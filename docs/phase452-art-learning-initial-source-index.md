# Phase 452 — Art Learning & Practice Studio Initial Source Index

**Purpose:** Provide a source-labeled, visitor-choice starting index for free art discovery, art-history study, creative practice, youth-safe learning, and AI-access research.

## Browser-verified visitor pathways

| Source | Public pathway confirmed | Studio label | Scope and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [The Met Collection][1] | Public collection search supports artist, title, date, object number, highlights, public-domain images, departments, research guides, and timeline pathways. | Available now | Individual object pages retain their own public-domain, copyright, cultural-sensitivity, and use context. |
| [Google Arts & Culture][2] | Public exploration provides art, museum, places, play, search, artist/discovery content, and a broad institutional discovery layer. | Available now | A cross-collection discovery route; individual partner records may carry different image, account, and display terms. |

## Initial Studio shelves

| Shelf | Public source path | Visitor purpose | Access label | Rights or access note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open museum records | [National Gallery of Art Open Access][3] | Discover collection records and designated open-access images for study and original response | Available now | Confirm rights on the specific object page; public-domain/open access does not apply to every work. |
| Open cultural collections | [Smithsonian Open Access][4] | Search public cultural records, images, and models for research and visual learning | Available now | Follow collection-specific context and reuse information. |
| Art history and close looking | [Smarthistory][5] | Read free art-history essays and visual-analysis resources | Available now | A contextual educational resource; follow its citations for primary-source study. |
| Modern-art learning | [MoMA Teaching Resources][6] | Explore educator-facing visual-literacy and creative-learning material | Available now | Some resources use downloadable documents and are structured for teaching settings. |
| Youth art exploration | [Tate Kids][7] | Explore free games, art activities, and creative prompts with adult-supported participation | Available now | Use with ordinary browser and adult-support considerations; do not create external accounts through ScrollVerse. |
| Youth art-learning resources | [Getty K–12 Learning][8] | Access art-history activities and classroom/home learning material | Available now | A resource path for learning; not a youth-submission or reward program. |
| Browser sketching | [AutoDraw][9] | Rapid visual ideation and browser drawing | Available now | Provider-operated tool; visitor checks current provider terms and privacy. |
| Browser visual planning | [Excalidraw][10] | Freeform visual notes, diagrams, and sketching | Available now | Suitable for planning and sketching rather than a full painterly studio. |
| Browser art practice | [Sketchpad][11] | Digital drawing and painting practice in a browser | Free exploration may be available | Provider features and access may change; visitor checks current provider terms. |
| Creative AI exploration | [Google Arts & Culture Experiments][12] | Explore arts-and-culture experiments involving creative technology | Available now | Experimental offerings may change; this does not create a shared or managed account. |
| Creative AI research | [Hugging Face Spaces][13] | Explore community-hosted AI and creative-tool demonstrations | Free exploration may be available | Individual spaces have separate terms, availability, queues, and output conditions. |
| Public art discovery | [Google Arts & Culture][2] | Search cultural collections, artists, themes, and related stories | Available now | Use individual record context before reusing or representing material. |

## Studio note

These destinations are external, source-labeled visitor choices. The Art Learning & Practice Studio can link to them, prepare original prompts, and offer browser-local project notes. It does not operate their accounts, capture a visitor’s external activity, claim a partnership, or promise traffic revenue, credits, rewards, grants, crypto incentives, or payment.
