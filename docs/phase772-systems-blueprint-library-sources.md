# Phase 772 — Systems Blueprint Library: Authoritative Foundations

## Purpose and boundary

This source record supports a browser-local comparative library for design patterns in trustworthy digital systems. It does not claim certification, standards-body membership, protocol adoption, interoperability with any external provider, agent deployment, or implementation of an external specification.

## Source-led design principles

| Source | Directly attributable principle | Browser-local blueprint translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) | NIST describes the AI RMF as voluntary guidance intended to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into AI design, development, use, and evaluation. Its framework uses the **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage** functions. | Keep purpose, sources, risks, limits, evidence checks, and next release condition visible in each ScrollVerse study record. |
| [NIST Artificial Intelligence](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence) | NIST emphasizes measurement, evaluations, benchmarks, tools, risk-based governance, and voluntary technical standards to promote trustworthy AI. | Treat system comparison as a measurement and design-learning exercise, not a performance or adoption claim. |
| [W3C AI Agent Protocol Community Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/cg/agentprotocol/) | The community group’s stated scope includes inter-agent communication, identity models, metadata formats, security and privacy mechanisms, and interoperability; it is a community-group exploration rather than a deployed protocol. | Record these as interoperability study prompts: identity, capability description, consent, authorization, privacy, and failure handling. Do not imply implementation or external agent collaboration. |
| [OECD AI Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/ai-principles.html) | The OECD principles promote human-centred, trustworthy AI and address inclusive benefit, rights and privacy, transparency, robustness and safety, accountability, traceability, and risk management. | Include a human impact, privacy, transparency, safety, accountability, and traceability check in every browser-local blueprint. |

## Reusable study cards

1. **Purpose and affected participants**: What is the system intended to do, who is affected, and who retains choice?
2. **Evidence and provenance**: What source supports the working statement, and what remains unverified or conceptual?
3. **Interoperability boundary**: Which information could be standardized, and which external protocol, provider, or agent relationship is not present?
4. **Evaluation and review**: What can be checked for grounding, completeness, privacy, safety, and usability before a next step?
5. **Reversible release**: What is browser-local now, what requires an owner release, and what would require independently documented external evidence?
