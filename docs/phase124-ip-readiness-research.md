# Phase 124 — Intellectual Property Readiness Research

## Purpose

This first-party research record supports ScrollVerse evidence organization, asset classification, and filing-readiness preparation. It is a working analysis, not legal advice, a filing, a clearance opinion, or a claim that a name, design, work, invention, or process is registered or exclusively owned.

## Core Distinctions

The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office explains that a trademark can protect names, logos, and other source identifiers used in advertising, and it highlights searching for similar marks, identifying goods/services, drawings, and specimens as part of the registration process. [1]

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that copyright protects original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium, including music, literary works, films, visual art, and software expression. It states that copyright generally exists upon fixation, while registration is required before an infringement suit for a U.S. work and can offer additional benefits. Copyright does not protect facts, ideas, systems, or methods of operation as such. [2]

The USPTO distinguishes patents from other IP: utility patents can cover new and useful processes, machines, articles of manufacture, or compositions; design patents can cover new, original, ornamental designs for articles of manufacture. Patentability has specific requirements and the agency recommends qualified patent-practitioner support because the process is complex. [3]

The USPTO describes a trade secret as information with actual or potential independent economic value from not being generally known, where reasonable efforts are made to keep it secret. Public disclosure can undermine a trade-secret strategy. [4]

## Working Protection Workflow

1. **Identify and separate.** Record each asset as original, commissioned, licensed, public-reference, submitted, or unknown.
2. **Preserve evidence.** Keep dated source files, creation logs, public release dates, contributor agreements, licenses, repository commits, and hashes.
3. **Classify the protection question.** Ask whether the asset is primarily a name/logo, creative expression, ornamental product design, technical invention, or confidential business information.
4. **Check exposure.** Note what has already been publicly disclosed and when; do not label something a trade secret after publishing its confidential details.
5. **Prepare—not file automatically.** Compile a reviewable asset packet, search record, specimen/deposit candidates, and counsel questions. No application is submitted without the owner’s explicit filing decision.
6. **Maintain the boundary.** Keep original ScrollVerse work separate from third-party inspiration, supplied links, and partner proposals.
