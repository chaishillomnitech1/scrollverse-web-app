# Phase 931 — Water Acoustics Experiment Brief

**Source basis:** Phase 930 receipt for `pasted_content_6.txt`  
**State:** Planning only; no experiment run and no result claimed  
**Purpose:** Convert the supplied water/frequency material into a reproducible, evidence-separated research brief.

## Evidence chain

> **Claim → Source → Experiment → Measurement → Reproduction → Status**

Every future observation should retain the exact source, instrument setup, raw data location, analysis method, replicate count, and status. A compelling narrative, visualization, or personal experience is not itself a measurement of a physical effect.

## Research question

How do ordinary acoustic signals transmitted through water change as controlled physical conditions vary, and can those changes be explained by established acoustic models before any additional interpretation is considered?

Claims about water memory, intention, Akashic records, ley lines, or consciousness remain hypotheses. They are not treated as established physics and are not implied by this brief.

## Variables to record

At minimum, record **frequency, temperature, salinity, pressure, dissolved gas, and container geometry**. Also record:

- source transducer model, drive voltage, waveform, duty cycle, and calibration date;
- hydrophone model, sampling rate, depth, orientation, and calibration;
- water volume, container material, dimensions, boundary condition, and mounting;
- ambient vibration, electrical noise, room temperature, and timing reference;
- trial identifier, operator, timestamp, blinded condition label, and replicate number.

Do not change multiple primary variables in one comparison unless the design explicitly calls for a factorial experiment.

## Minimal safe design

1. Write a preregistered question and expected measurable outcome before collecting data.
2. Use a stable container, fixed source and sensor geometry, and a calibrated signal chain.
3. Randomize condition order and use blinded labels where practical.
4. Include baseline water and sham/no-drive trials.
5. Use repeated trials, preserve raw waveforms, and report exclusions before looking at the final comparison.
6. Compare measured amplitude, phase, attenuation, resonance peaks, and timing against an ordinary acoustic model.
7. Separate exploratory plots from confirmatory tests and report null or ambiguous results.
8. Reproduce the strongest observation with fresh water, a fresh session, and a second operator or instrument when feasible.

Any physical setup should follow appropriate laboratory, electrical, pressure, and water-handling safety practices. This brief does not authorize construction, energization, high-pressure operation, or exposure to hazardous equipment.

## Analysis plan

Use a fixed preprocessing record: calibration correction, filtering, windowing, Fourier or time-frequency transform, and noise-floor method. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only images or peak values. A result is **reproducible** only when the same procedure and independent repeat produce compatible measurements within a declared tolerance.

Use three status labels:

- **Observed:** recorded in a defined setup but not yet independently repeated.
- **Model-consistent:** compatible with an established acoustic/physical explanation under the recorded conditions.
- **Exploratory hypothesis:** an interpretation requiring a new test; not a validated physical conclusion.
