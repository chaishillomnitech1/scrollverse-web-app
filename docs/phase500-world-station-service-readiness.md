## Research-informed design standard

The World Station is a public-knowledge-first service studio. Its purpose is to let a visitor inspect reusable methods for websites, systems, artifacts, research interfaces, and delivery blueprints; prepare a local project record; and identify the exact consent and release evidence required before any external action is considered.

Accessibility is treated as an engineering and content requirement rather than a decorative enhancement. WCAG 2 documents apply to web content, mobile, multimedia, and AI web interfaces, and organize testable success criteria around content that is perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust.[1] The World Station therefore retains visible operating states, keyboard-reachable controls, plain-language choices, source labels, and a correction path.

Privacy is handled through minimization and named boundaries. NIST describes its Privacy Framework as a voluntary tool to help organizations identify and manage privacy risks while building products and services.[2] The World Station will not collect customer contact, payment, credential, or confidential material in its browser-local brief. Any later service engagement requires a separate, explicit data boundary.

The public-knowledge path remains open. UNESCO describes open access as free access to information and unrestricted use of electronic resources, subject to the applicable rights and attribution conditions.[3] The World Station therefore directs visitors toward free learning, source trails, and reusable method patterns while keeping protected or commercial delivery work in a separate release-controlled lane.

## Service lanes and evidence fields

| World Station lane | Publicly inspectable method | Browser-local record fields | Requires separate owner release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Websites and experience systems | Information architecture, accessible interaction, source and correction controls | Scope, intended audience, accessibility needs, destination, acceptance criteria | Building, publishing, hosting, analytics, account or domain changes |
| Creator catalog and media | Release packets, media evidence, delivery/support planning | Authorized asset, source, rights/contributor status, accessibility, delivery criteria | Product creation, licensing, distribution, customer terms, payment or checkout |
| Knowledge and research interfaces | Source taxonomy, study pathways, correction and limitation labels | Research question, source class, attribution, open-access status, limits | Content publication beyond available rights, provider connection, data collection |
| Operations and service blueprints | Role map, evidence path, accountable review and correction | Objective, roles, data boundary, support record, acceptance criteria | Client engagement, contract, price, external implementation or automation |
| Integration and exchange readiness | Compatibility questions, action gates, audit and stop conditions | Provider or destination, intended output, consent, records, stop condition | API connection, marketplace, search service, wallet, Web3, payment, or transaction |

> A ready brief is not permission to create a client site, send a proposal, schedule a job, collect a lead, set a price, publish an artifact, connect a provider, or initiate an exchange. Each requires its own exact record and current authorization.
