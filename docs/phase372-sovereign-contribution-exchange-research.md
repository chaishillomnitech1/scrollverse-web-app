# Phase 372 — Sovereign Contribution and Co-Creation Exchange Research

## Research use

This research informs the design of a creator-owned visitor pathway. It does not make ScrollVerse a government, a public institution, a legal governance body, a financial intermediary, or an authority over a contributor. The applicable practical ideas are transparent opportunities to participate, an understandable decision process, a reasoned response path, inclusive access, and clarity about what a contribution does and does not create.

## Design findings

| Research finding | Exchange design application |
|---|---|
| Ongoing dialogue works best when the opportunity, rules, process, and progress are easy to find | The Exchange presents its lanes, operating states, local-only planner, and connected routes together. |
| Inclusive participation requires clear information, adequate time, and more than one way to engage | Visitors can learn, use a browser-local planner, read community routes, or prepare a collaboration brief; no payment or public post is required for access. |
| A reasoned response creates accountability for why an idea moves forward, changes, or does not proceed | Future submitted proposals will require an owner-defined intake and response record rather than silent acceptance or automated action. |
| Contributions are not limited to code or money | The Exchange recognizes ideas, writing, research, design, moderation, feedback, translation, organization, creative production, and support as different potential contribution types. |
| Meaningful participation involves real decision context, not a decorative ballot | The page links to existing governance and assistive-governance routes as context and does not pretend a browser-only tool has made a binding collective decision. |

## Source-specific guidance

The Open Government Partnership’s participation and co-creation standards emphasize accessible process information, inclusive opportunities to give input, ongoing dialogue, transparency, and clear responses to stakeholder priorities. [1] The Open Source Guide explains that valuable contributions can include writing, design, organizing, moderation, translation, user research, tutorials, and mentoring—not only code. [2] The Participatory Budgeting Project describes participatory democracy as processes that put decision-making power in community hands and frames co-governance as power shared between an entity and community participants. [3]

> The practical standard for ScrollVerse is simple: publish the opportunity, explain the boundaries, welcome multiple forms of participation, label what is local versus live, and give every later activated process a response and correction path.

## Implementation rules

The first Exchange release will remain browser-local for the idea and co-creation planner. A contributor will be told not to enter sensitive personal information, credentials, account data, payment information, private contract terms, or proprietary material they do not have permission to share. A later server-side intake, vote, trade, payment, or distribution feature requires its own source, privacy, rights, consent, and explicit owner-release design.
