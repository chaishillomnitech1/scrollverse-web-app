# Phase 371 — Smooth Exchange and Commerce Ethics Guide

## Purpose

The ScrollVerse Direct-to-Fan Catalog is designed to make a future exchange clear, accessible, privacy-aware, and creator-directed. This guide applies before any individual item is activated. It does not activate payment, determine a price, or replace item-specific release, delivery, refund, or professional review requirements.

## Five smooth-exchange practices

| Practice | Visitor experience | Catalog implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Clear offer before commitment | A visitor can see the exact item, edition, source state, what is included, total price/fees when an offer exists, and delivery expectations before checkout | The item-level release brief requires exact title/version, source, package, price, fulfillment, and support fields before activation. |
| Voluntary, reversible navigation | A visitor chooses an offer without hidden activation, forced enrollment, or a disguised paid action | The current catalog is browse-only; no payment, token, wallet, membership, or delivery action is attached to a page control. |
| Privacy and payment separation | Sensitive payment data is handled by an approved payment processor rather than copied into general page inputs or project notes | Future payment work uses a separately confirmed processor-hosted or low-risk collection flow. Browser-local release notes warn against entering credentials, card data, account numbers, customer data, or private agreements. |
| Accessible, understandable interaction | The reader can use keyboard controls, visible focus states, plain labels, readable text, and clear status language | The catalog uses real buttons/labels, named controls, contrast-aware cards, no time-limited purchase action, and operating-state labels. |
| Honest delivery and support path | After a future exchange, the visitor receives an accessible confirmation, delivery status, and contact path rather than an ambiguous promise | Every future item must record its exact fulfillment and customer-support method before it can become an offer. |

## Research basis

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission’s online marketing guidance emphasizes disclosure of deal details up front and includes guidance for online sales, delivery promises, advertising, reviews, and transparent fees. [1] Stripe’s integration-security guide explains that organizations handling payment data must work in a PCI-compliant manner and recommends lower-risk integrations that collect payment information directly through Stripe instead of through the merchant’s server. [2] WCAG 2.1 identifies perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust interaction as foundational accessibility principles and includes requirements for alternatives, input purpose, focus/keyboard use, and status communication. [3]

> A smooth exchange is not only a fast checkout. It is a readable offer, voluntary choice, safe collection method, accessible interface, accurate fulfillment, and a correction path when something changes.

## Activation checklist for a future item

Before a test-mode payment and delivery review, the owner-controlled release record must include the exact item title/version, asset source, rightsholder and contributor context, authorized offer description, approved price and disclosed fee treatment, delivery timing or access method, refund/support route, and approved payment-destination configuration. A live exchange should use the approved payment processor and must not expose secret keys, raw card data, customer data, wallet credentials, or private contract material in the client interface.
