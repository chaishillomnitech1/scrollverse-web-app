# Phase 443 — ScrollVerse Knowledge Commons Source Standard

**Purpose:** Establish the shared source, translation, access, rights, and correction rules for a free, reader-directed ScrollVerse library spanning gnostic materials, comparative religious texts, lost-text research, ancient archives, historical leaders and thinkers, inventions, institutions, and financial history.

## Library principle

The Knowledge Commons presents sources so visitors can read, compare, trace, and decide. A source is not made into a universal conclusion simply by being displayed. Each record makes its access path, edition or translation, scope, date, and limitations visible.

> **Free access to knowledge does not require flattening the source, erasing its context, or copying a work that is not lawfully available for full reproduction.**

## Record taxonomy

| Library lane | Typical source object | Default visitor label | Required context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gnostic and related early religious materials | Codex, manuscript page, scholarly edition, translation, museum/archive record | Research | Manuscript/codex or edition, translator/editor where named, language, access limits |
| Comparative religious and scripture study | Primary text, translation, commentary, academic catalog | Available now or research | Tradition, edition/translation, language, purpose, and reading path |
| Lost-text and fragment research | Fragment, archaeological catalog, manuscript witness, reconstruction study | Research | What survives, what is reconstructed, source institution, and unresolved gaps |
| Ancient world archives | Inscription, tablet, papyrus, public-domain translation, excavation catalog | Research | Artifact or text identity, collection/archive, date convention, translator/editor, access state |
| Leaders, thinkers, inventors, and institutions | Primary writing, patent, archival record, official biography, museum or library collection | Available now or research | Named person/institution, source organization, record type, date, and limitation |
| Financial history and economic thought | Central-bank, government, archive, historical publication, scholarly source | Research | Historical period, institution/author, source date, and non-advisory boundary |

## Source hierarchy

| Priority | Use | Examples of acceptable record types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Primary or custodial source** | First choice when publicly accessible | National libraries, museums, university collections, government archives, central banks, manuscript repositories, patent offices |
| **2. Scholarly edition or translation** | Used when the primary artifact is not readable to most visitors | University press edition page, institutional digital edition, named translator or editor, peer-reviewed catalog |
| **3. Public-domain or openly licensed reading copy** | Used for direct reading only when its access rights are clear | Institutional open-text collection, public-domain scan, open-license translation |
| **4. Contextual secondary source** | Used to orient a visitor or compare interpretations | Museum essay, university guide, documented scholarly reference |

## Access and rights labels

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Available now** | A verified public reading, viewing, or source page is reachable from the library record. |
| **Linked reading** | The library links to an external source; it does not reproduce the work in full. |
| **Translation comparison** | Two or more named translations or editions are linked for reader comparison. |
| **Research** | A source or inquiry path is available, while its interpretation, reconstruction, or access status remains open. |
| **Requires owner release** | A ScrollVerse-authored adaptation, paid edition, or original commentary is not yet approved for public release. |

The library may summarize a source in original language, identify the source, and provide a link. It will not reproduce full text, scans, translations, images, audio, or other protected material unless the access right, license, or public-domain status supports that use.

## Minimum source-card fields

Every Knowledge Commons card must include the fields below.

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Title and lane | Clear resource title and library category |
| Source organization or edition | The custodian, publisher, archive, named translator, or editor where available |
| Public destination | Direct public URL for reading, viewing, listening, or inspecting the source |
| Source date or period | A source publication date, artifact period, or stated uncertainty |
| Access label | Available now, linked reading, translation comparison, research, or requires owner release |
| Scope note | What the visitor is being shown and what the record does not establish |
| Rights/access note | Full-text availability, public-domain/open status where known, or link-only treatment |
| Correction path | A visible way to report an inaccurate title, link, attribution, access state, or source description |

## Comparative and historical care

Religious, gnostic, and lost-text material must be presented with its named source and edition instead of being made to speak for an entire tradition or community. Ancient text fragments and reconstructions must make surviving evidence and uncertainty visible. Historical leaders, conquerors, thinkers, inventors, financial institutions, and economic ideas should be linked to documentary records and context; the library does not turn historical material into endorsement or instruction.

Financial-history records are educational context, not personalized investment, token, credit, payment, or trading recommendations. A visitor should be able to explore the source without being guided to a financial transaction.

## Correction and revision

If a public URL breaks, a translation is misidentified, an access right changes, an attribution needs correction, or a source description overstates what the record shows, the card returns to **research** until corrected. The correction log retains the source of the change and the current operating label.
