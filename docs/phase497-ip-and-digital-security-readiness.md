## Asset-protection record

The IP and Digital-Security Readiness route separates five asset lanes: creative works; names, logos, and other brand identifiers; inventions or technical methods; confidential methods or business information; and names, portraits, voices, or likenesses. Each lane requires a source/version record, contributor or custodian context, scope, intended use, unresolved evidence field, and correction path. It does not treat a portal label, hash, repository, source image, public post, or physical possession as a substitute for the relevant rights, clearance, official record, or purpose-specific authorization.

| Record lane | Core evidence question | Not established by a source alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative work | What original fixed work, version, creator/contributor context, and release or license record exists? | Registration, title, contributor clearance, license, or public-display authorization. |
| Mark or brand identifier | What exact mark, goods/services context, jurisdiction, and observed search/record date are documented? | Availability, registration, clearance, priority, or enforceability. |
| Invention or technical method | What dated technical disclosure, contributor context, novelty question, and jurisdiction review are recorded? | Patentability, application status, scope, ownership, or enforceability. |
| Confidential information | What access purpose, custodian, permitted-recipient record, and protection process apply? | That confidentiality has been preserved or that a trade-secret claim exists. |
| Likeness or brand-use matter | What source, depicted person/mark, intended context, audience, territory, duration, and permission/review record apply? | A reusable likeness, publicity-rights permission, endorsement, or brand-use authorization. |

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that copyright protects original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium, while names, titles, short phrases, ideas, systems, and processes are treated differently; it also distinguishes automatic copyright protection from registration.[1] The USPTO distinguishes the roles of trademarks, patents, and copyrights: trademarks identify the source of goods/services; patents address qualifying technical inventions; and copyrights protect original artistic, literary, and intellectually created works.[2]

## Digital-security control expectations

The route records review questions for secret isolation, access and role control, encryption in transit, encryption at rest, audit/correction records, and recovery/rotation. NIST describes its Cybersecurity Framework as a resource for helping organizations understand and improve cybersecurity risk management.[3] These controls are presented as verification needs, not as a claim that a control exists, is sufficient, is certified, is “military-grade,” uses a particular key length, or is operationally configured.

| Control | Required evidence before a positive claim | Current portal state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secret isolation | Protected secret-management location, authorized owner, redaction review, incident path | No public secret intake; implementation evidence required. |
| Access control | Role matrix, purpose, least-privilege approach, review and revocation record | Implementation evidence required. |
| Encryption in transit and at rest | Observed configuration, provider/system scope, data classification, owner, and verification date | Configuration verification required. |
| Audit, correction, recovery, and rotation | Audit location, correction route, retention decision, recovery owner, rotation trigger, and protected runbook | Browser-local planning available; protected review required for implementation. |

## External-action boundary

The route does not file a copyright, trademark, patent, or any other registration; determine rights or legal clearance; make a licensing or enforcement claim; transmit a credential or secret; configure a security control; issue a token; connect a wallet; or change a provider. Those actions require a specific asset, jurisdiction, record, destination, consequence, and exact current owner confirmation.
