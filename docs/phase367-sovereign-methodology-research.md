# Phase 367 — Sovereign Methodology Learning References

## Research purpose

This source set supports a creator-authored open record without conflating a personal account, an original work, a public source, and an externally testable claim. The methods are presented as free learning pathways for documenting provenance, evaluating material, and interpreting language with awareness of context.

| Learning lane | Source-led practice | Application in the portal |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance | Record origin, date, author or agent, method, transformation, and version for an item. | Each source record identifies what it is, where it came from, when it was reviewed, and how it is being used. |
| Reproducibility | Preserve method, tool, data, and process context so a reader can examine a result. | Local project validation is recorded separately from copied metrics or claims. |
| Source evaluation | Distinguish background, exhibit, argument, and method sources; trace claims back to original context. | A channel screenshot, public channel page, owner statement, and research method remain separate record types. |
| Interpretation | Examine language, intention, historical context, and the relation of a part to a larger work. | First-person accounts and language study are displayed as interpretations, with sources available for reader-led comparison. |

The Australian Research Data Commons describes data provenance as documentation of a data item’s origin and of the processes and methodology by which it was produced; it recommends fields such as creation date, creator, tools, and processing methods. [1] The ProvCaRe research paper describes provenance information as supporting the traceability of origin and history in reproducibility work, and identifies method, tools, and data as useful categories for recording study context. [2] The University of San Francisco’s research guide describes the BEAM source-function model and SIFT’s steps to investigate a source and trace claims, quotations, and media to their original context. [3] The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes hermeneutics as the study of interpretation and emphasizes that understanding remains open to reconsideration through attention to context and presuppositions. [4]

## Portal method derived from the sources

For each ScrollVerse record, the portal will identify the source type, capture or review date, author or contributor role when known, version, operating state, method, and correction path. Personal accounts will be labeled as first-person records; public materials will link to their original accessible source; and claims requiring external verification will remain open for source comparison rather than being elevated by a label alone.
