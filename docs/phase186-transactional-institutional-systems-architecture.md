# Transactional Exchange and Institutional Systems Atlas Architecture

The public atlas separates four views: **system purpose**, **role allocation**, **evidence and control questions**, and **risk/resilience learning**. It uses only source-led educational patterns and browser-local planning; it offers no financial functionality.

| Atlas layer | Learning question | Not offered by the route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator release | What is the work, product, buyer promise, delivery path, and rights basis? | Sale, checkout, payment acceptance, product listing, or marketplace matching. |
| Provider role | Which external party would actually process, distribute, host, support, or custody a live service? | Provider selection, account onboarding, connection, credential entry, or provider representation. |
| Evidence/control | Which facts, records, terms, risks, privacy practices, authorizations, and support procedures need review? | Legal, tax, security, financial, compliance, or consumer-protection determination. |
| Token concept | What source, rights, utility, buyer-promise, code/security, and activation questions remain? | Wallet connection, token issuance, mint, exchange, custody, trading, price quote, or investment statement. |

The Atlas draws public learning distinctions from payment-system and financial-market-infrastructure sources. That contextual use does not make ScrollVerse a financial institution or participant in payment, clearing, settlement, custody, or exchange operations. [1] [2] [3]
