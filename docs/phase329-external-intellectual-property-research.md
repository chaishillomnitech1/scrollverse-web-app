## Primary-Source Findings

The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office distinguishes trademarks, patents, and copyrights by the kind of subject matter they protect: source identifiers, technical inventions, and original fixed creative works, respectively. The U.S. Copyright Office provides registration resources for creative works, while trade-secret protection depends on valuable information remaining nonpublic and being subject to reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy. [1] [2] [3]

| Protection lane | What the official sources describe | ScrollVerse stewardship record before any filing or public claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copyright | Original creative works fixed in a tangible medium, including books, music, films, software, photographs, and artwork. [1] [3] | Final asset/version, authors/contributors, fixation/source date, chain of creation, accessible materials, intended release destination, and owner decision. |
| Trademark | Words, phrases, designs, or combinations that identify and distinguish goods or services; federal registration is tied to particular goods/services and source identification. [1] | Candidate name/logo/slogan, goods/services description, public-use status, screening/search record, design source, related mark-risk notes, and filing decision. |
| Patent | New, useful, and eligible technical inventions/processes; patent protection has distinct subject-matter and process requirements. [1] [2] | Invention disclosure, technical problem/solution, drawings or method, contributor records, confidentiality/public-disclosure history, prior-art review queue, and patent-practitioner review decision. |
| Trade secret | Information with actual or potential economic value from not being generally known, where reasonable efforts maintain secrecy; all elements remain necessary. [2] | Secret inventory, authorized-access list, confidentiality terms, storage controls, disclosure log, training/handling instructions, and an explicit decision to retain secrecy rather than publicly disclose. |
| License and collaboration | Ownership and permissions must be specific to the asset and intended use. | Rights holder, scope, territory, media, term, exclusivity, attribution, sublicense rule, payment/royalty terms if any, accessibility delivery, and signed/evidenced agreement record. |

## Filing-Readiness Sequence

The USPTO frames a practical IP journey as **search, apply, and maintain**. A creator-facing tool should therefore separate a private preparation queue from a public claim: first identify the actual created work or invention; then conduct appropriate screening; then prepare an application or agreement through the relevant official path; then record the actual filing/registration or agreement outcome. [2]

> A concept is not automatically a patent, trademark, copyright registration, trade secret, or enforceable license simply because it is written down. The record should show what exists, who created it, what was kept confidential, what was publicly released, and what formal outcome—if any—was obtained.

## Practical Boundaries for ScrollVerse

The local portal can provide a stewardship map, intake forms, source/version logs, accessibility checks, rights matrices, and correction paths. It should not state that a name is cleared, a mark is registered, an invention is patentable, a work is owned free of competing claims, or a trade secret is protected without the relevant evidence and review.

The most important operational choice is often between secrecy and disclosure. Once material intended to be confidential is broadly disclosed, trade-secret treatment may be affected; a planned public release should therefore be recorded separately from confidential invention or method records. [2]
